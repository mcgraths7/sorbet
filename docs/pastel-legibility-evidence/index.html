The numbers on every sample

  • Ink is the text colour. A family is one hue of ink at several lightnesses.
  • Lc is "lightness contrast", the score from APCA (the Accessible Perceptual Contrast Algorithm, the contrast measure drafted for the next version of the web accessibility guidelines). It says how strongly text stands off its background by brightness alone, and brightness is the part of contrast that colour blindness leaves largely alone: under the simulations these scores move by 1 to 5 points. Black on white is about 106; under about 15 is effectively invisible.
  • What the measure's authors publish as minimums: 90 for paragraphs from 14px regular up (they call 90 "preferred"); 75 for paragraphs only if the type is at least 18px regular or 16px medium (medium is one step heavier than regular); 60 for text that is not a paragraph, such as headings and labels, and only when it is at least 16px bold, 18px semi-bold or 24px regular; 45 for big headings, meaning 24px bold or 36px regular; 30 is their floor for placeholder and disabled text. These are their minimums for a general audience and the method is still a draft. None of it is a measurement of your eyes or your screens, so your eye wins.
  • Old rule is the contrast ratio from WCAG (the current web accessibility guidelines) that sorbet enforces today: at least 4.5:1 for text, on a scale from 1:1 (no contrast) to 21:1 (black on white).
  • Lightness, colourfulness, hue are the three dials of OKLCH, the colour model the palette is built in. Lightness runs from 0, black, to 1, white. Colourfulness is how far from gray: 0 is gray, these pastels are 0.06 to 0.09, and a vivid colour is 0.15 or more. Hue is an angle round the colour wheel: about 30 is red, 90 yellow, 150 green, 200 a green-blue, 260 blue, 310 purple.
  • Distance between two colours (the tables below) is measured in a colour space called OKLab, times 100: under 5 is hard to tell apart, 10 or more is clearly different.
  • A simulation is a calculation of how a colour looks with one kind of colour blindness: protan and deutan are the two red-green kinds, tritan is the rare blue-yellow kind. These use the complete form of each kind, which is the worst case. Most colourblind people have a partial form, so what you see is probably better than these columns say.
  • For comparison, sorbet's text today is #38342f: lightness 0.33 · colourfulness 0.010 · hue 74°, a dark gray, Lc 93 on today's page. Sorbet's "muted" text today is Lc 79 and its "subtle" text Lc 69, which you already read every day as secondary text in the lab apps.

  • One ink is the anchor. The darker label shade, the lighter secondary shade and the placeholder shade are all worked out from the ink you pick, so changing your mind later is one value.

5 · Edges, the three treatments

You chose E2. Sheet 2 shows it across real states and in dark mode, so this section is only the record of the three. None uses a gray or black line. The edge colour round cards and fields is a caramel, #a16e32: the cream taken darker and a little toward orange (hue 68 against the cream's 88). The edge of a button is its own colour taken darker, for example #7f5fab under the lilac button. Checkboxes and switches keep a firm ring in a darker lilac, #b096d7, in all three, because a box or a switch has no text inside it and its outline is the only thing showing where it is. Only the card and the buttons change between the three treatments; the field changes only in E3; checkboxes, switches and the divider lines are the same in all three.
